The Ramayana is traditionally divided into seven distinct books or chapters, known as Kandas. These Kandas chronologically trace the life of Lord Rama, moving from his miraculous birth to his eventual ascension and departure from the mortal world.Here is the detailed breakdown of the seven Kandas of the epic:1. Bala Kanda (The Book of Youth)This book focuses on the early life, divine origins, and education of Rama and his brothers.Key Events: King Dasharatha performs the Putrakameshthi sacrifice to obtain children, resulting in the birth of Rama, Lakshmana, Bharata, and Shatrughna.Major Milestones: The sage Vishwamitra takes the young prince Rama and his brother Lakshmana into the forest to fight predatory demons like Taraka. They travel to Mithila, where Rama strings the heavy bow of Shiva, winning the princess Sita in marriage.2. Ayodhya Kanda (The Book of Ayodhya)This chapter details the political turmoil and emotional crisis within the royal court of Ayodhya.Key Events: King Dasharatha decides to crown Rama as his successor, causing joy across the kingdom. However, Queen Kaikeyi is manipulated by her maid Manthara.Major Milestones: Kaikeyi uses two ancient boons to demand the banishment of Rama for 14 years and the coronation of her son, Bharata. Rama voluntarily accepts exile to protect his father's honor. Sita and Lakshmana join him, leaving the city in deep mourning, which leads to Dasharatha's death from heartbreak.3. Aranya Kanda (The Book of the Forest)This text marks the transition of the narrative from a palace drama into a spiritual wilderness quest filled with supernatural challenges.Key Events: Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana live a peaceful, ascetic life in the Dandakaranya forest, interacting with sages and protecting them from demons.Major Milestones: The demoness Shurpanakha tries to seduce Rama and attacks Sita, prompting Lakshmana to cut off her nose. In revenge, her brother Ravana, the king of Lanka, uses a magical golden deer disguise to lure the brothers away and abducts Sita.4. Kishkindha Kanda (The Book of the Monkey Kingdom)Set in the monkey kingdom of Kishkindha, this chapter introduces Rama's key military allies.Key Events: Searching desperately for Sita, Rama and Lakshmana meet Hanuman and form an alliance with Sugriva, the exiled monkey king.Major Milestones: Rama kills Sugriva’s tyrannical brother, Vali, restoring Sugriva to the throne. In gratitude, Sugriva mobilizes vast search parties of Vanaras (monkeys) to look for Sita across all directions, culminating in Hanuman learning that Sita is trapped across the southern ocean.5. Sundara Kanda (The Book of Beauty)This book is named after Hanuman’s nickname, "Sundar," and centers entirely around his heroic exploits and pure devotion.Key Events: Hanuman expands his physical form and makes a miraculous leap across the ocean to the island fortress of Lanka.Major Milestones: He locates a grieving Sita in the Ashoka grove, reassures her by presenting Rama's signet ring, and assesses Ravana's military power. Before leaving, he allows himself to be captured, and when the demons set his tail on fire, he escapes to burn down a significant portion of Lanka.6. Yuddha Kanda / Lanka Kanda (The Book of the War)This is the longest book of the epic, detailing the massive military campaign and the final clash between good and evil.Key Events: The Vanara army constructs a floating bridge (Rama Setu) across the ocean to reach Lanka. A catastrophic war ensues, featuring legendary battles with Ravana’s titans, including Kumbhakarna and Indrajit.Major Milestones: Rama slays Ravana using the divine Brahmastra arrow. Sita is rescued but undergoes a trial by fire (Agni Pariksha) to publicly prove her purity. The 14-year exile concludes, and they return victoriously to Ayodhya, where Rama is crowned king.7. Uttara Kanda (The Book of the Aftermath)This final chapter explores the events following Rama's coronation, dealing with deep themes of duty, governance, and public perception.Key Events: As king, Rama hears citizens murmuring doubts about Sita’s long captivity in Lanka. Prioritizing his absolute duty as a ruler (Raja Dharma), he makes the agonizing choice to exile a pregnant Sita to the forest.Major Milestones: Sita finds refuge at the hermitage of sage Valmiki and gives birth to twins, Lava and Kusha, who grow up learning the Ramayana poem. Years later, they sing the epic before Rama. Sita returns to the court, calls upon Mother Earth to swallow her as proof of her ultimate innocence, and Rama eventually relinquishes his mortal form, returning to his divine state as Vishnu.
